Important Security Update — Malakh 1.0.5 and Earlier Versions

Version 1.0.5 includes improvements to peer authentication, contact verification, secure session handling, and reliable Bluetooth message delivery. These improvements have undergone developer testing, including physical-device tests. Malakh has not undergone an independent third-party security audit.

Warning for version 1.0.2: Matching verification emojis in that earlier version do not reliably establish the identity of another person or device. Different identities or sessions can produce matching symbols, and earlier trusted-contact/session authorization had limitations. A nearby active attacker could potentially impersonate a contact during connection establishment. Encryption by itself does not remove that risk.

If you are still using version 1.0.2, do not treat matching emojis or an existing trusted-contact indicator as conclusive proof of identity. We recommend avoiding sensitive or confidential communications through that version.

Recommended action: Update both devices to Malakh 1.0.5 or later through Google Play when the update is available. Carefully compare the verification information displayed on both devices before approving a contact. Older and newer versions may not be able to connect to one another; do not bypass security checks to force a connection.

The protections available depend on the version actually installed on each device. Updating this policy does not upgrade the app or resolve vulnerabilities in older installed versions. No confirmed incident of unauthorized message interception has been established in the developer's investigation to date.

1. Overview

Malakh is an offline nearby communication application. Its core messaging operates directly between nearby devices without requiring an internet connection or a developer-operated central messaging server.

This policy describes how Malakh handles information, the security features and limitations of different versions, and the choices available to users.

2. Data Handling and Messaging

  • No Accounts: Malakh does not require user accounts, phone numbers, or email sign-ins.
  • No Central Servers: The developer does not operate a central messaging server that receives users' messages, peer identities, or contacts.
  • No Analytics or Ads: Malakh does not include advertising SDKs, tracking pixels, or analytics trackers in the release described by this policy.
  • Local Content Storage: User-created message content is stored locally on the sender and recipient devices in Android-protected application storage.
  • Peer Communication: Messages are transmitted directly between nearby devices using Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE). Communication involves exchanging the protocol and identity information necessary to establish a connection.
  • Recipient Device Autonomy: Information delivered to another user's device is controlled independently by that device's user.
  • Support Communications: If you contact us by email, we receive the email address and information you voluntarily provide in that communication. Please do not send private cryptographic keys or sensitive conversation contents in support requests.

3. Security and Authentication

  • Message Encryption: Malakh uses authenticated encryption to protect message content during established secure peer sessions. Encryption does not, by itself, prove that the peer is the person you intend to contact.
  • Peer Authentication and Verification: Version 1.0.5 includes changes to cryptographic identity authentication and binds verification approvals to current authenticated peer/session information. Users should compare the verification information displayed on both devices before approving a new or re-verified contact.
  • Older-Version Verification Warning: In version 1.0.2, the emoji-based Short Authentication String (SAS) is not reliably bound to the complete authenticated identity and session. Matching emojis in that version must not be relied on as trustworthy proof of a peer's identity.
  • Legacy Contacts and Changed Identities: Contacts carried forward from older versions may require a new verification comparison before trusted communication resumes. If another device presents a different cryptographic identity, Malakh may treat it as a new contact and create a separate conversation rather than automatically attach old history or trust.
  • Public Identifiers: Public identity information and cryptographic public keys may be exchanged between devices during connection establishment.
  • Private Keys: Private identity-key material is intended to remain protected on the user's Android device and is not sent to a developer-operated messaging server.
  • Security Testing and Limitations: Version 1.0.5 has been tested by the developer, including security-related automated tests and physical-device messaging tests. It has not received an independent third-party security audit. No software can guarantee absolute protection, and Malakh must not be relied on for emergency communications.

4. Device Permissions

  • Bluetooth Permissions: Nearby Bluetooth permissions (Scan, Connect, Advertise) are used for peer discovery, advertising, connection, and direct communication.
  • Legacy Location Requirement: On Android 11 (API level 30) and earlier, Android may require Location permission for Bluetooth LE scanning. Malakh uses BLE scan results to discover nearby peer radios and does not use these results to derive, record, or retain physical location.
  • Unrequested Permissions: The Malakh messaging functionality described here does not require contacts, microphone, camera, or shared external media storage permissions.

5. Retention and Data Deletion

  • Data Retention: Local information remains in protected application storage until removed.
  • Deletion Controls: Malakh 1.0.5 does not provide a dedicated in-app control for deleting one individual chat. Users may remove local Malakh data through Android Settings by clearing the app's storage, or by uninstalling Malakh. These are broad device-level actions, not single-conversation deletion controls.
  • Deletion Consequences: Clearing application storage or uninstalling may permanently remove local messages, drafts, identity keys, trusted-contact records, and other app information. A new installation may generate a new cryptographic identity, and former contacts may need to verify it again.
  • Recipient Copies: Clearing local information does not remove copies of messages previously delivered to another user's device.
  • No Central Message Deletion: Because the developer does not operate a central message database, the developer cannot remotely delete messages independently stored on another user's device.
  • Support Data Requests: For questions about personal information voluntarily provided through support communications, contact malyilmathew@gmail.com .
  • No Data Sales: No Malakh user data is sold or provided to advertisers, analytics companies, or data brokers.

6. Security Measures and Children's Privacy

  • Technical Security: Malakh uses encrypted peer sessions and device-local identity protection mechanisms. Version 1.0.5 introduces improvements intended to address the earlier contact-verification and trust-authorization limitations documented for version 1.0.2. The older version remains affected until it is updated.
  • Security Updates: Security improvements apply only to the version installed on a user's device. Both participants should use the latest available compatible Malakh version and compare verification information when prompted. Some older and newer versions cannot communicate with one another.
  • Children's Privacy: Malakh is a general communication application and is not specifically directed to children. Parents and guardians should consider its privacy and security limitations before permitting use.

7. Updates and Contact

Changes will be reflected by updating the revision date at the top of this page. Updating this document does not, by itself, change the security characteristics of previously installed Malakh versions. Please use Google Play to obtain the latest available app update.
