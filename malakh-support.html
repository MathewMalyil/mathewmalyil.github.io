Malakh is an offline nearby communication application. Core messaging operates directly between nearby Android devices using Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), without mobile data, Wi-Fi internet access, or a developer-operated messaging server. Both users need Malakh installed and Bluetooth enabled, and they must remain within Bluetooth range.

Security and Version Compatibility

Important for users of version 1.0.2: The earlier emoji-based Short Authentication String (SAS) had known contact-verification limitations. Matching emojis alone must not be treated as reliable proof of the other person's identity.

Malakh 1.0.5 introduces improvements to peer authentication, verification, session establishment, and message-delivery reliability. Always compare the complete verification information shown on both devices and approve a contact only when it matches and you trust the person you are verifying.

Older Malakh versions may discover newer versions but fail to establish a compatible secure connection. Update Malakh on both phones through Google Play when the newer version is available. Do not bypass identity verification to make an incompatible connection work.

These improvements apply only to devices running the updated app. Malakh has not undergone an independent third-party security audit and should not be used for emergency communications.

Troubleshooting Nearby Connections

1. Grant Nearby Permissions

In Android Settings > Apps > Malakh > Permissions, allow the nearby-device permissions requested by Malakh for Bluetooth scanning, connection, and advertising. On Android 11 and earlier, Android may also require Location permission for Bluetooth scanning.

2. Check Bluetooth and Distance

Turn Bluetooth ON on both phones and keep them within nearby Bluetooth range. If discovery or connection stalls, check that Bluetooth is enabled and open Malakh on both devices.

3. Use the Latest Compatible Version

On both phones, open Google Play and check Malakh for updates. Different protocol versions may appear in the Nearby list but not connect securely. Updating both devices is preferable to repeatedly attempting a connection between incompatible versions.

4. Compare Complete Verification Information

When Malakh asks you to verify a contact, compare the complete verification information displayed on both phones with the person physically present. Do not rely only on matching emojis from older versions. If the information differs or the identity is unexpected, do not approve the connection. A contact with a newly generated identity may require fresh verification and appear as a separate conversation.

5. If a Chat Shows Reconnecting

Malakh can reconnect automatically after some Bluetooth interruptions, but this is not guaranteed in every situation. Allow a short period for recovery. If it remains Offline or Reconnecting, open the Nearby screen; you may need to tap Connect. After reopening the app, manual connection may be required. Keep both apps open during troubleshooting.

6. Understand Message Delivery Indicators

A message may be queued while a secure connection is unavailable. A single tick indicates transmission to the nearby device, not necessarily that the recipient has saved it. Double ticks indicate that Malakh has received the recipient's durable application acknowledgement. If a message remains undelivered, check both devices' connection and version status.

Messages, Chat History, and Updates

A normal Google Play app update is intended to retain local conversations, drafts, and identity records. For the same authenticated peer identity, Malakh is designed to reuse the existing conversation. If another phone has regenerated its cryptographic identity, it may appear as a new verified contact and have a separate conversation; the earlier history remains associated with the previous identity.

Do not uninstall Malakh or clear its app data to troubleshoot a chat. Doing so may permanently remove local messages, drafts, trusted contacts, and identity information. Reinstalling is not a reliable way to recover old conversations.

Deleting Local Information

Malakh 1.0.5 does not currently provide an individual Delete Chat control. Android's Clear Storage/Clear Data option or uninstalling Malakh can remove local app information, but these actions may also remove the device's Malakh identity, trust records, and all locally stored conversations. They should not be used merely to remove one chat.

Removing local information does not delete messages already stored on another person's device. Malakh does not operate a central message database from which recipient copies can be deleted remotely.
